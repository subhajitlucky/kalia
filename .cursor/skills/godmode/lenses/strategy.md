# Strategy lens — product, organizations, negotiations

Use for plans, launches, positioning, negotiations, org design, and decisions
with people and incentives.

## Frame

- What is the actual objective, and who owns it?
- What does success look like in 90 days, one year, and beyond?
- What are the constraints: money, people, time, attention, trust, law?
- What is the cost of failure, and who bears it?

## Map the game

- Who are the stakeholders? What do they want, fear, and control?
- What are the incentives? What does the system reward, regardless of what it
  says?
- Who is the decision-maker, and who can veto?
- What is the binding constraint? What would change if it disappeared?
- What is the competition, including “do nothing” and “the spreadsheet”?

## Options

- At least three: the obvious, the do-nothing, the inverse, the constraint
  removal, and the 10× version.
- For each: cost, upside, downside, reversibility, time to signal.
- Prefer the reversible, cheap-to-test option with an asymmetric upside.
- What is the smallest decisive step that produces information?

## Execution

- Who does what by when?
- What is the leading indicator, not the lagging one?
- What is the first 48-hour action?
- What is the pre-mortem: why will this fail?
- What is the rollback or exit?

## Communication

- Answer first; then the reasoning.
- One message, one decision, one owner.
- Separate the recommendation from the analysis.
- Say what you would need to change your mind.

## Biases

- Planning fallacy, sunk cost, consensus bias, status quo bias, and the
  temptation to optimize the metric instead of the goal.
- A strategy that depends on everyone behaving perfectly is not a strategy.
