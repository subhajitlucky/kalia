## X16 — result: REJECTED (negative result, published)

Pre-registered 2026-09-23 (LEDGER `a1ebbf6c…`). Four arms: control vs
chunk-preserving reversal (50% of windows, chunks 4–16), two seeds each,
500 steps, then the Abhimanyu gap on each final checkpoint.

| Arm | val loss @500 | forward NLL | reverse NLL | Abhimanyu gap |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ctl-s1337 | 3.7924 | 4.0700 | 9.2314 | 5.1614 |
| ctl-s1338 | 3.9708 | 4.0345 | 9.0945 | 5.0600 |
| rev-s1337 | 3.8698 | 4.0963 | 9.3066 | 5.2103 |
| rev-s1338 | 4.0583 | 4.1343 | 9.4170 | 5.2827 |

Seed-pair means: control gap **5.1107**, treatment gap **5.2465** (Δ +0.136,
wrong direction); control val **3.8816**, treatment val **3.9641** (Δ +0.083).

- P1 (control gap > 0.3 nats): **CONFIRMED** — the entry–exit asymmetry is
  already ~5.1 nats at 30M parameters, consistent with 6.06 nats at 58M.
- P2 (gap closes by ≥ 0.10): **FAILED**.
- P3 (forward loss within +0.02): **FAILED**.

Both seeds moved the same wrong direction on both metrics.

**Interpretation:** reversing chunk *order* while preserving intra-chunk order
does not transfer to token-level reversal. The transform mildly hurts. A
stronger form (token-level reversal, or a lower reversal ratio) is a separate
hypothesis for a later cycle, not a silent retry.

## I14 — the published training log was incomplete for two days

Found while monitoring v0.2.0: `kalia-lm/kalia-v012`'s `logs/train_log.csv` began
at step 1740, so sessions 1–2 of v0.1.2 (347 logged steps, the whole descent
from 10.7 to the plateau) were missing from the public record.

**Root cause (timestamps, not speculation).** Log persistence on resume
(`train.py`, restoring the CSVs from the hub before appending) landed in commit
`3afb435` at 2026-09-23 06:00 UTC. v0.1.2's session 1 ended at 05:10 UTC (last
push `f093c311`) and session 2 started immediately after — running the pre-fix
code, which truncated the log at 1740. Session 3 ran the fixed code, pulled
session 2's log, and appended. So the *only* lossy session was session 2, and the
cause was a mid-run code change, not a bug in the current code.

**Recovery.** HF keeps per-file commit history, so the lost epoch was
reconstructed from the repo itself: 173 rows at revision `f093c311` (steps
10–1730) plus 174 rows at HEAD `650ea5e1` (steps 1740–3470). The merge is
contiguous — 347 rows, steps 10→3470, monotonic, no step gap other than the
10-step log interval. The val log was recovered the same way (13 points, steps
250→3250, revisions `3f8a82bf` + `650ea5e1`).

Both merged files are now in `docs/curves/` and have been **republished** to
`kalia-lm/kalia-v012` (`logs/train_log.csv`, `logs/val_log.csv`). The overwrite
is strictly additive: the previous file is a subset and remains in the repo's
commit history either way.

**What the recovered curve adds.** The val log now shows the part that was
missing, and it supports D41 rather than complicating it: 4.0881 (250) → 3.2859
(500) → 2.9836 (750) → 2.8100 (1000) → 2.6108 (1250) → 2.5270 (1500), then
oscillation inside 2.40–2.63 for the remaining 1,750 steps with a single best
point (2.3986 at step 3,000). The descent is real and early; the last third of
the schedule is a plateau. Stopping at 3,478 kept the plateau, not the gains.

**Process change.** A resumed session now restores its logs before appending, and
this incident is the reason that behaviour is treated as load-bearing rather than
incidental: a log gap is invisible until someone diffs the curve against the
schedule. Verification added: after every session, check that the published
`train_log.csv` starts at step 10 and its last step matches the checkpoint.
