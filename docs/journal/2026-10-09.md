# Journal — 2026-10-09

A day of firsts: the Kautilya policy ran for the first time, and the RCAA
design died by test before any GPU hour.

## X26 pilot — the Kautilya policy runs, after two DDP bugs

Launched mid-morning (2x T4, DDP, paired arms from v0.2.0 at step 4770, seed
1401, 1000 update steps). The static arm completed — final val **2.8200**
against v0.2.0's 2.8248 — and its full trajectory is archived in
`docs/results/logs/x26-kautilya-pilot.log`.

The adaptive arm failed twice before running clean:

- **v1: CUDA OOM** inside `per_source_loss` (batch 16 x context 1024 logits
  are ~3 GiB, on a card already holding training state). Fixed by chunking the
  eval forward into micro-batches with `empty_cache`; a test pins that the
  chunked and full-batch numbers agree (they differ only by float summation
  order, 2.4e-7).
- **v2: a 600-second NCCL deadlock** after the first reweight. Two real bugs
  in the never-exercised DDP reweight path: (1) rank 1 stored the broadcast
  tensor as a **CUDA** `_probs` while the sampler draws with a CPU generator;
  (2) the broadcast was sent **before** the master's update, so non-master
  ranks sampled a stale mixture until the next reweight — the one thing the
  broadcast exists to prevent. Fixed with `set_probabilities` (CPU storage), a
  post-update broadcast, and a barrier.

v5 then ran through every reweight. The diary shows the level-based fallback
"eating dessert": it keeps downsampling the hard source (FineWeb-Edu
60% → 49% by decision #3) and upsampling the easy ones (TinyStories 20% → 30%,
Python 5% → 7.5%) because it judges by confusion *level*, not by improvement.

**The trend policy was therefore finished the same day** — the A1 rule as
designed (improving → dana, flat → sama, worsening → bheda, collapsed → danda),
with history, a relative trend threshold, and config wiring. The pilot ran the
weaker fallback; the trio (static vs level vs trend) is queued for the reset
week.

## X27 — registered, then withdrawn the same day: the reverse stream leaks the future

RCAA was registered under the D48 protocol in the morning: the Abhimanyu gap is
5–6 nats, 20–50x the measured seed spread, so unlike every arm D48 closed, it
is legitimately screenable.

The first implementation plus a causality test found the flaw the design could
not: **the reverse stream at position `p` attends to original positions
`q >= p`** — flipping makes the causal mask anti-causal in real coordinates.
Position `p` feeds the prediction of token `p+1`, which the reverse stream has
just read. The registered R-P2 (forward val ≤ control + spread) would have
passed spectacularly, for entirely the wrong reason.

Withdrawn before any GPU hour (amendment 1, ledger `X27-am1`). The config flag
is now a tripwire that raises with the reason; the non-leaking repair
(reverse-order aggregation of the causal prefix) carries no information the
forward stream does not already have and is deliberately not run. The gap stays
open; a backward-prediction head or a non-autoregressive class are the
surviving directions, each needing its own design.

This is the fourth silent-null-class defect caught before quota was spent
(D51, I19, I20) and the first that lived in a *design* rather than in code.
Its signature was the opposite of the usual one: not a mechanism that never
switched on, but one whose numbers would have looked too good.

## X26 pilot: finished — the dessert, confirmed

Both arms completed (adaptive, the fourth attempt after the OOM and the NCCL
deadlock; static, in the archived first run). The paired design did its job:
the arms tracked each other to **0.002 nats** while the policy barely moved,
then diverged exactly as the mixture drifted.

**Held-out val at step 5700: static 2.8200, adaptive 2.8377 (+0.0177).** A
registered screen, not a verdict — the paired-update noise is unmeasured and a
same-config rerun plus a fresh seed are required before any reading. But the
two robust findings do not depend on that delta:

1. **The drift is monotonic and total.** Ten decisions, every one the same
   direction: FineWeb-Edu 60% → 22.7%, TinyStories 20% → 56.3%, Python 5% →
   14.1%. The policy downsampled the hardest source every time.
2. **It gamed its own metric.** Training loss fell to **1.79** while held-out
   loss on the original distribution did not improve — the policy lowered the
   number it optimizes by changing the distribution that number is measured
   on. "Eating dessert", demonstrated at full strength.

Artifacts in `docs/results/x26-pilot/`; results README updated. The
trend-based policy (judge by improvement, not level) is implemented and is the
natural next arm — the pilot is exactly why it exists.

Also today: `entity_decode.py` (rule-based entity verifier over best-of-N) was
built and tested, waiting for its measurement run.
