## Entity-verified decoding: first measurement, and it works

20 frozen Gutenberg stories, 8 samples each, the same pool ranked three ways
(`docs/results/entity-decode.json`):

| selector | retention | paired Δ vs greedy |
|---|---|---|
| greedy | 0.030 | — |
| log-probability pick | 0.035 | +0.006 [−0.012, +0.023] |
| **entity pick (rule)** | **0.091** | **+0.061 [+0.039, +0.086]** |
| reference prose | 0.098 | — |

The deterministic rule triples retention over greedy and beats the
log-probability selector 2.6x; the log-probability selector's gain is again
zero-consistent. The model's entity-picked continuations keep their characters
about as well as the actual book text does.

Two caveats, recorded beside the result: the metric rewards keeping names, not
quality (the model beats the reference on several stories because real prose
moves on while the model echoes); and the rule trades likelihood for
retention — entity picks average −2.23 log-prob against greedy's −0.38. They
are still the model's own samples, but whether story quality pays for the
consistency is unmeasured, and it is the next question.

First new capability result of the reset week, and the first time in this
project a decoding-time mechanism moved its target metric by more than the
noise floor — the delta is 6x the 58M seed spread, paired over 20 stories.
