## Entity-verified decoding: first measurement, and it works

20 frozen Gutenberg stories, 8 samples each, the same pool ranked three ways
(`docs/results/entity-decode.json`):

| selector | retention | paired Δ vs greedy |
|---|---|---|
| greedy | 0.030 | — |
| log-probability pick | 0.035 | +0.006 [−0.012, +0.023] |
| **entity pick (rule)** | **0.091** | **+0.061 [+0.039, +0.086]** |
| reference prose | 0.098 | — |

The deterministic rule triples retention over greedy and beats the
log-probability selector 2.6x; the log-probability selector's gain is again
zero-consistent. The model's entity-picked continuations keep their characters
about as well as the actual book text does.

Two caveats, recorded beside the result: the metric rewards keeping names, not
quality (the model beats the reference on several stories because real prose
moves on while the model echoes); and the rule trades likelihood for
retention — entity picks average −2.23 log-prob against greedy's −0.38. They
are still the model's own samples, but whether story quality pays for the
consistency is unmeasured, and it is the next question.

First new capability result of the reset week, and the first time in this
project a decoding-time mechanism moved its target metric by more than the
noise floor — the delta is 6x the 58M seed spread, paired over 20 stories.

**Correction (2026-10-21).** The selector maximises `retention` and the kernel
reports `retention`, so the 0.061 is the rule grading its own paper. The
holdout test in `docs/results/entity-holdout/` selects on the first half of each
continuation and scores the second half the selector never saw: **+0.0108,
95% CI [−0.0073, +0.0318]**, not distinguishable from zero. The 0.0907
full-text number reproduces, which is what a circular selector should do. The
coverage result survives — the oracle reaches 0.0618 on the held-out half
against greedy's 0.0170 — but the "triples retention" claim does not, and this
entry's last paragraph overstates what the measurement established.

## X28 static + level finished: the dessert replicated, the machine is exact

**Run-to-run:** the static arm reran the X26 control and matched it to
**+0.0001 nats at the final checkpoint** (2.8201 vs 2.8200; per-checkpoint
agreement −0.0032 to +0.0019). The paired-noise bound X26 deferred is now
measured, and it is tiny.

**Replication:** the level arm (the X26 policy, rerun) ended at **2.8384**
against X26's adaptive **2.8377** — **+0.0007** — with a diary identical to
the X26 diary to four decimals. The policy's cost on held-out val:
**+0.0183 nats** (X26: +0.0177), growing with the drift from +0.0001 early to
+0.0183 late. Against ~0.001–0.003 session noise, the cost is readable by an
order of magnitude or more.

So the first real Kautilya finding is now: **a self-referential mixture policy
that judges sources by loss level drifts monotonically to the easy ones, its
training loss plummets (2.63 → ~1.79), and held-out loss on the original
distribution is consistently ~0.018 nats worse — replicated across two
independent sessions.** Fresh-seed replication (1402) is still required by the
pre-registration before any claim.

Slots refilled immediately: **trend** (does judging by improvement stop the
drift?) and **x29-uniform** (the first patha arm, the control) are now running.

## X28 trend finished: the drift slowed, not stopped — and the cost tracked it

Final held-out val: static **2.8201** < trend **2.8277** < level **2.8384**,
and the mixture drift orders identically (FineWeb 60% fixed / 41.4% / 22.7%;
TinyStories 20% / 41.4% / 56.3%). The trend rule — judge by improvement, not
level — **halved both the drift and the cost**, and the registered mechanism
prediction (weights stay near 60/20/15/5) is falsified: the drift continued,
just slower.

Recorded hypothesis for why: both rules share one feedback loop — more
exposure lowers a source's loss (familiarity, not learning), and
lower/improving loss earns more exposure. `per_source_loss` draws windows
from the same shards the sampler trains on, so the signal cannot tell learning
from familiarity. The fix a next iteration would need is a frozen per-source
probe set never trained on; not queued.

The dose-response is the useful part: three arms, three drift magnitudes,
three costs, ordered — the drift causes the cost, not merely accompanies it.
