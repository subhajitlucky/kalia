## X17 matched rerun: the fix is free, and the benefit is not detectable

The first X17 run measured the treatment **+0.0444 nats worse**, but both arms
were evaluated with full causal attention: the control was trained and tested
matched, the treatment was tested in a regime it had never trained in. A 2×2 on
the archived checkpoints isolated the mismatch at −0.0570, but that was measured
on final losses only, with a non-registered protocol.

`evaluate()` was fixed to apply the attention mask a masked run trained under
(`9e3cc83`), and the registered four-arm, two-seed run was repeated. The curves
in `docs/results/x17-matched/` give:

| bar | measured | verdict |
|---|---|---|
| DM-1 mean paired Δ ≤ −0.010 | **−0.0051** | FAIL |
| DM-1b both deltas negative | **+0.0031 / −0.0133** | FAIL — seeds disagree in sign |
| DM-2 ahead at ≥3/5 points, both seeds | 0/5 and 5/5 | FAIL |
| DM-3 step time ≤ 1.25× | ×1.048 | PASS |
| DM-4 mask engagement < 1.0 | 0.8061 | PASS |
| DM-5 paired spread | 0.0164 | reported |

**The +0.0444 was an artifact. The benefit is not detectable at this scale.** The
2×2's matched mean (−0.0124) was premature and is retracted: the registered
protocol puts the same contrast at −0.0051, with the seeds disagreeing in sign.
The fix is correct and costs 4.8% step time; it simply does not measurably help,
so there is no case for changing the recipe.

## Entity decoding: the recorded 3× is the selector grading its own homework

The project's one measured inference-time win was entity-verified decoding:
retention 0.0907 against greedy's 0.0296, +0.061, with the human reference at
0.0978. It is the basis for the memory-only rung.

It is also close to circular. `entity_decode.select_best_index` picks the
candidate maximising `(retention, mean_span, logprob)`, where `retention` comes
from `eval_entities` — and `eval_entities.retention` is the metric the kernel
then reports. The measurement is "a selector that maximises X achieves a high X".

What is genuinely established is narrower: the good continuation **is in the
sample set**, and the model's own probabilities cannot find it (logprob-pick
0.0353 against the oracle's 0.0907). That is a statement about coverage, about
selection being the bottleneck rather than generation.

The test that breaks the circularity is `kalia-entity-holdout`: split every
continuation at its midpoint, let the selector see only the first half, and
measure retention on the second half it never saw. The kernel reports greedy,
logprob-pick, half-pick (the test), full-pick (the recorded number, for
continuity), oracle (coverage) and the human reference, paired per story, with a
bootstrap CI over stories. It runs on CPU so it costs no quota.

It crashed on its first run — `encode_ordinary(...)` returns a list, not a
tensor, and `.tolist()` on the slice died after story 0's work. The fix adds a
fail-fast tokenizer probe, incremental writes after every story, and invariants
that make a wrong number loud (the oracle must bound every selector). The first
successful run was then canceled at **11 of 20 stories**; the partial is
consistent with the circularity diagnosis (half-pick 0.0293 against greedy
0.0270, full-pick 0.0603, oracle 0.0627), but it is not a result. Relaunched
2026-10-21 on CPU.
