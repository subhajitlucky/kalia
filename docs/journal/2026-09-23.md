## Research sweep (frontier labs → our scale)

Sources: DeepSeek-V3 report, Kimi K2 report (MuonClip), GLM-4.5 report,
MiniMax-01, Xiaomi MiMo-7B, modded-nanogpt records, and 2026 optimizer papers
(Muon+, NorMuon, Polar Express, U-NorMuon/Aurora), IMU-1 (430M small-model
recipe), L20-Edu-135M (negative RLVR result at 135M), LADDER/SGS/FlowBalance
(self-improvement frameworks).

Key conclusions:

1. **Optimizer frontier moved past plain Muon**: Muon+ (one post-polar
   normalization step, zero extra state) beats Muon, NorMuon, AdaMuon and
   Turbo-Muon from 60M to 7B parameters — our exact size range — with reported
   speedups up to 37%. Polar Express coefficients and cautious weight decay are
   cheap complements.
2. **Small-model architecture stack** (IMU-1, 430M): QK-Norm (have) + per-head
   attention gating + value residual connections + LayerNorm scaling, with
   NorMuon + cautious WD + checkpoint EMA → 5.2% relative loss reduction at
   matched tokens.
3. **RSI is real but scale-gated**: self-play/RLVR frameworks (LADDER, SGS,
   FlowBalance) need verifiable rewards and ≥1B models; a 135M model showed
   GRPO on GSM8K *degrading* accuracy (1.82% → 1.59%). Our RSI path must be
   data-side (self-training with filters) and automation-side (agent-run
   experiment loops), not RL-first.
4. **Cheap final-quality wins**: post-hoc checkpoint EMA during decay, WSD
   schedule with flexible decay timing, batch-size warmup.

## Punch-above-weight research
- Modern sub-1B models train at 10,000–30,000 tokens per parameter; KALIA is at
  43. That is the dominant capability gap — architecture is secondary.
- Adopted levers: ~10% code mixing (~2× effective tokens), depth over width,
  GQA, block-wise layer sharing, data quality (Cosmopedia), WSD + EMA +
  target-loss stopping. Queue X1–X5 in `docs/research/2026-09-23-technique-backlog.md`.
- **Distillation rejected (D21)**: KALIA remains a pure from-scratch lineage.
  The resulting capability ceiling is accepted knowingly, in exchange for an
  auditable "every weight is ours" claim.
