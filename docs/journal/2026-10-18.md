was supposed to reproduce, the probe was verified against its own anchor to the
last decimal — and the retention instrument then contradicted the capability
instrument about the one thing the replication existed to settle.

Direction reproduces on val. The margin does not: at s1402 the replay advantage

## The probe could not score the replication as written

The CL-0 kernel inferred its arm set from mount-path text — baseline was "every
`ckpt.pt` without `/x29-` in it", an arm's name came from `re.search(r"x29-([a-z]+)")`.
The X28 static arm writes `out/kautilya-static/ckpt.pt`, which matches neither, so
attaching it made the baseline assertion count two and the run refused to start.
**The static arm has never been scored on CL-0 at either seed**; the no-replay side
of the contrast did not exist on this instrument.

Fixed in `fc19ecd`. The arm set is now declared per cohort, the resolver refuses a
missing/ambiguous arm, a baseline that is not one file, and any attached checkpoint
no arm declares, and `resolve_cohort` takes its mount points as arguments so the
decision that picks what gets scored is testable off-Kaggle (17 tests). The cohort
`x29-replication` carries the seed-1401 anchors: without `static-s1401` the
no-replay side would have no row to pair with, and `uniform-s1401` is the
within-session anchor. Also fixed there: the code-snapshot glob matched every
attached kernel's own copied code tree, and taking `hits[0]` was correct only
because `datasets` sorts before `notebooks`.

## What the two instruments say

Both instruments are reproducible across seeds; they disagree with each other.

- **val** favours replay, by +0.0125 (s1401) and +0.0073 (s1402) — the second
  inside the static arm's own seed spread.
- **CL-0** favours no-replay, by 0.0884 (s1401) and 0.0939 (s1402) — 22× the
  largest seed spread measured in this cohort (0.0040), and the same direction at
  both seeds.

The replication's own ruler is much tighter than the pretraining ruler: seed
spreads here are 0.0015–0.0070 nats, against the 58M control spread of 0.2892
(D48's finding) that stopped the architecture line. A 1000-step continual update
from a shared checkpoint washes out most seed dependence.

## Two confounds, both on the record before the result was read

**1. The two arms differ in more than replay.** The caveat was written into the
cohort table before the run, and it now carries the result rather than annotating it:

| | corpus | passes over it | policy |
|---|---|---|---|
| uniform | 2.4B `train.bin` + 10% replay shard | ≈0.2 | — |
| static | 4 × 124,500,691-token equalised shards at 60/20/15/5 | ≈1.05 | `adaptive_every: 0` |

Both arms spend ~524M tokens (1000 steps × 8 × 32 × 1024 × 2). The no-replay arm
therefore makes ~5× more passes over its corpus. `replay_prob` defaults to 0.0 and
the Kautilya runner passes no `--replay-bin`, so "static" genuinely has no replay —
but "replay" is not the only variable that moved.

**2. `corpus/probe_val.bin` is a frozen prefix of the training stream.** Not a
held-out set: `tools/build_publish_sources_notebook.py` reads the first 1M tokens
of the v2b `train.bin` and publishes them, deliberately — *"A held-out set would
measure nothing."* That is sound for detecting forgetting of what was learned, but
loss on a fixed training slice also moves with how hard an arm fits that slice,
which is exactly what differs between these two arms.

**Not concluded:** that replay harms retention. The CL-0 gap co-moves with the
delivery path and with corpus passes, so it is not attributable to replay alone.

## The M0 gate

The plan's gate was *"uniform 10% replay + rewarm beats no-replay beyond the
spread"*, read on both instruments, deciding whether the v0.2.1 export/eval/publish
sequence runs. On CL-0 the contrast reverses well beyond the spread. **The gate is
not passed; the v0.2.1 sequence does not start on this evidence.** The X29
"shuffled replay helps" reading — which was always a val-only claim — survives on
`val.bin` and does not survive on the retention ledger.

## Correction: the published shard notice described the probe wrongly

`docs/legal/corpus-shard-notice.md` line 15 listed `probe_val.bin` as

## Later the same day: the no-replay control, and replay was never doing the work

`x29-noreplay` ran the X29 runner at seeds 1401/1402 with `REPLAY = 0.00` —
same runner, same 2.4B `train.bin`, same 1,000-step budget as `uniform`, so
replay is the only variable that moves. Both COMPLETE.

**Canonical val, step 5700:**

| arm | replay | corpus (passes) | s1401 | s1402 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| uniform | 10% | 2.4B (~0.2) | 2.8076 | 2.8058 |
| **noreplay** | **0%** | 2.4B (~0.2) | **2.8064** | **2.8078** |
| static | 0% | 498M (~1.05) | 2.8201 | 2.8131 |

The contrast decomposes, and the decomposition is the result:

- **replay's own effect** (uniform − noreplay, delivery path held fixed):
  **+0.0012** at s1401, **−0.0020** at s1402. The sign flips between seeds and
  the magnitude is inside the uniform arm's own seed spread (0.0018).
- **the delivery path's effect** (noreplay − static, replay held at zero):
  **−0.0137** at s1401, **−0.0053** at s1402, consistent in sign.

So the uniform−static gap that this morning's entry read as "replay helps"
(+0.0125 / +0.0073) is the corpus and the passes over it, not the replay. On
the val instrument **10% replay + rewarm does not beat no-replay beyond the
spread — it does not beat it at all, and it does not reliably beat it in
either direction.**

This was the reading the caveat predicted, and it is worth stating plainly
because the earlier entry could not: the confound was not a technicality that
might have mattered, it was the entire effect.

### The same contrast on CL-0, and the two instruments agree about the big thing

`kalia-x29-noreplay-probe` scored all four train.bin arms on the pinned probe
(sha `1664f71a…` asserted at run time; the v0.2.0 self-check re-scored 2.798875
against the recorded 2.798875 at **delta 0.000000**; both uniform anchors
reproduced their 2026-10-18 rows exactly, 2.775169 and 2.776687).

| arm | CL-0 probe_loss | drift vs baseline |
|---|---|---|
| uniform-s1401 (10% replay) | 2.775169 | −0.023706 |
| uniform-s1402 (10% replay) | 2.776687 | −0.022188 |
| **noreplay-s1401** (0%) | **2.769569** | **−0.029306** |
| **noreplay-s1402** (0%) | **2.771140** | **−0.027735** |
| static-s1401 (0%, shards) | 2.686720 | −0.112155 |
| static-s1402 (0%, shards) | 2.682753 | −0.116122 |

The uniform→static gap that this morning's entry reported as the day's result
decomposes almost entirely into something other than replay:

| seed | uniform−static | replay's part | path's part |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1401 | 0.088449 | **0.005600 (6.3%)** | 0.082849 (93.7%) |
| 1402 | 0.093934 | **0.005547 (5.9%)** | 0.088387 (94.1%) |

**Replay is 6% of what this morning looked like the headline.** The other 94% is
the delivery path — the no-replay arm it was read against samples a 498M-token
corpus at 60/20/15/5 about 1.05 times, while these arms sample the 2.4B
`train.bin` about 0.2 times.

What is left is small but not noise: replay's own CL-0 cost is +0.005600 and
+0.005547, consistent in sign and near-identical in size at both seeds, against
a measured seed spread of 0.0015 for either arm — about 3.6× the ruler. So on
the retention instrument 10% replay is *mildly harmful*, reproducibly.

The two instruments now agree on the large thing and still differ on the small
one. Both say the path dominates. On replay's own effect, val finds nothing
(+0.0012 / −0.0020, sign-unstable, inside the 0.0018 spread) while CL-0 finds a
consistent small penalty. That disagreement is not resolved here, and it is the
kind that the four-metric protocol exists to keep visible rather than average
away.

**M0's gate — "uniform 10% replay + rewarm beats no-replay beyond the spread" —
is not passed.** On val it does not beat it at all; on CL-0 it loses, beyond
the spread, at both seeds. Whatever v0.2.1 turns out to be, it is not justified
by this contrast.
