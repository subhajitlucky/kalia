## X37 — the Pāṇinian rule grammar does not beat a matched MLP

The SUTRA micro finished: 6 rule arms at seeds 1338/1339, with the valid v1
base/control reused after the 3D-Newton–Schulz optimizer bug. ~14 GPU-h across
both launches.

| arm | val s1338 | val s1339 | mean |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| control (matched MLP) | **4.0692** | **4.2290** | **4.1491** |
| rules | 4.1503 | 4.2113 | 4.1808 |
| full | 4.1721 | 4.2564 | **4.2143** |

- **S-1 FAIL** — full − control **+0.0652**.
- **S-2 FAIL** — swap deltas **−0.00128 / −0.00059**; the full is *less*
  sensitive to the name swap than the control.
- **S-3 PASS** — entropy 4.157, max share 0.018, effective **63.9 / 64**.
- **S-5** — full − rules val **+0.0335**; the carry/scope is worse than the
  rules alone on val.

**`advance_full = false`.** The codebook and load balance work; the rules are
used **uniformly**, so the codebook is acting as capacity, not as a specialized
grammar. The dense control is better on val and at least as good on the swap.
No 58M scale.

The one positive is real: the router does not collapse, and all 64 rules are
live. That is the clue for the next attempt — **specialization**, not more
rules.

## "Should we stop?"

No. A failed **registered hypothesis** stops; the programme does not. SUTRA's
particular implementation — 64 top-1 rules, a 1,024-wide general path, 763
steps — is a null. The idea of a learned rule grammar is not disproved; the
evidence says this version spent capacity without producing specialized rules.

The next attempt must change one thing and be pre-registered:
- rule specialization/diversity (the uniform router is the failure signal);
- a general path matched to the control's 1,408 width;
- a longer 1,500-step micro;
- or role-conditioned routing.

The X36 memory has the same shape: retention passes, language cost fails. The
next version needs a val-preservation mechanism, not a re-tune.
