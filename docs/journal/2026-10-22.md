# Journal — 2026-10-22 (the entity heads are necessary, not the switch)

The first mechanistic probe. The question was whether one attention head hides
the name the model already knows. The answer is no: the entity information is
concentrated in layer 9, but ablating those heads makes retention worse. The
selection failure is not a single switch to turn off.

## X32 — the entity-selection circuit

Registered before the run (`2026-10-22-X32-entity-selection-circuit.md`). Twenty
stories, eight samples, one greedy draw; a manual attention path recorded the
mass from continuation positions to the prompt's entity tokens; the top three
heads by enrichment were ablated, with a random-three control and a reference
NLL guard.

| layer | head | entity mass | enrichment |
|---|---|---|---|
| 9 | 3 | 0.01201 | **3.45×** |
| 9 | 2 | 0.00826 | **2.37×** |
| 9 | 7 | 0.00741 | **2.12×** |

Those are the only heads above the pre-registered 2× bar. H1 passes: entity
information is concentrated.

**H2 fails.** Ablating the top three heads changes second-half retention by
**−0.0117** [−0.0263, +0.0000] against greedy. The heads are **necessary**, not
harmful. The random-three control is −0.0052 [−0.0291, +0.0199]; top-minus-random
is −0.0065 [−0.0284, +0.0124]. The NLL guard passes at **+1.05%**, so the
ablation did not destroy the model.

**Registered verdict: no single-head intervention.** The entity heads carry the
name, but removing them does not reveal the right one. The selection failure is
either downstream at the readout, or distributed across heads. A pilot cannot
distinguish those yet.

## The prior-art check, before the next claim

The broad claims are not world-first. The internet check found:

- continual pretraining and replay are heavily studied — TiC-LM (2025),
  Lifelong Pretraining (2022), Investigating Continual Pretraining (2024),
  Learning Dynamics in CPT (2025), REMIX (2024), FoMo-in-Flux (2024);
- the selection/verifier bottleneck in small models is known — Small Language
  Models Need Strong Verifiers (2024), Learning Generative Selection for
  Best-of-N (2026), Best-of-Evidence (2026), the compositionality gap (2023);
- small-model continual-learning benchmarks exist.

What may survive as first is narrower: a **controlled sub-100M decomposition of
replay versus delivery path**, and a **coverage–selectability measurement at
58M**. The broad “no one noticed” framing does not survive.

## What follows

No more head ablation. The next mechanistic test is a **linear probe on layer-9
residual states**: can a simple readout rank the candidates better than the
model's own log-probabilities? If yes, the information is linearly decodable and
the failure is at the readout. If no, it is distributed or absent. Activation
steering is the second test; more ablation is not.

The bigger programme remains the same: the model's knowledge is not the
bottleneck; selection and delivery are. X32 narrows where to look.
