# Amendment 1 to X19 — correct G-2's expected parameter count

Registered: 2026-09-28, **before** the `micro-branchnorm` arm has been trained
and before any GPU time was spent on it. The original X19 registration is
hash-anchored at
`036d0981fd83c8cc98bdf793f78f6463b0d8db7f0a9541dfa236fc7599b27f3e`
(`docs/preregistrations/2026-09-28-X19-branch-norm-isolation.md`).

## What is being corrected

G-2 was registered as:

> **G-2** | The branch norm is free | `micro-branchnorm` params **== 29,920,512**,
> exactly equal to control

**That is arithmetically wrong**, and it was wrong in the direction that made
the arm sound better than it is. `RMSNorm` carries a learnable `weight`
(`model.py`: `self.weight = nn.Parameter(torch.ones(dim))`), so the four
per-branch norms are not free — they add `4 × branch_dim = n_embd` parameters
per block.

The correct expectation at the micro dimensions (`n_embd 384`, `n_layer 6`):

| arm | params | vs control |
|---|---|---|
| control | 29,920,512 | — |
| **branchnorm** | **29,922,816** | **+2,304** (6 blocks × 384) |
| gated | 30,072,768 | +152,256 |

**G-2 is restated as:** `micro-branchnorm` params **== 29,922,816**, and the
gate's own contribution is the difference between `gated` and `branchnorm`, which
must be **149,952** — the `w1`/`w2` matrices of the gate and nothing else.

The gate's share decomposes as `6 × (384·32 + 32) + 6 × (32·384 + 384) = 6 ×
24,992 = 149,952`, i.e. both matrices plus both biases across six blocks.

**A second arithmetic slip in the first draft of this amendment** put that figure
at 150,952. It was caught by instantiating all three arms and counting
parameters rather than trusting the arithmetic on paper — `29,072,768 −
29,922,816 = 149,952`. The figure below is the measured one, and the
verification is now part of the test suite rather than a one-off check. Both
slips were in the direction of making the arm look cheaper or tidier than it is,
which is the specific failure mode pre-registration exists to catch.

## G-2 and G-3 verified against the implementation, before any GPU

Both registered correctness checks were run locally against the constructed
models, so neither can be a surprise on Kaggle:

```
control     29,920,512
branchnorm  29,922,816   (+2,304)
gated       30,072,768   (+152,256)
gate-only contribution (gated - branchnorm) = 149,952   PASS
```

G-3 is asserted in `test_branch_norm.py`: after the branch norm, every quarter of
the stream has RMS ≈ 1.0, while the control's quarters retain whatever spread the
raw stream has — a >1% difference, which is what makes the arm a real change
rather than a near-identity.

## Why the correction strengthens the prediction rather than weakening it

The substantive claim of G-2 was never "the arm is free" — it was "**the gate's
+0.5% of parameters is not what earns the −0.0436 nats**". That claim is
untouched, and the restatement measures it *more* precisely: comparing
`gated` against `branchnorm` isolates the gate's parameters exactly, whereas
comparing against `control` confounds the gate with the norms.

It is also worth stating the size of what is at stake. The gate costs 150,952
parameters for a mechanism G-0 has now measured as non-responsive to its input
(input spread 5e-06 against a mean of 0.019). If `branchnorm` reproduces the
gain, those 150,952 parameters are being spent on a constant.

## Why the error happened, and why it is recorded rather than quietly fixed

G-2 was written from the design description — "four independently normalised
slices concatenated back" — without checking whether the normalisation in
`model.py` is affine or non-affine. It is affine. The pre-registration is
supposed to be the artefact that makes exactly this kind of unverified arithmetic
visible before it can flatter a result, and here it did the opposite: the error
would have made a real finding look cleaner than it is.

**The pre-registration's own rule is that a change to a threshold requires a
hash-registered amendment.** G-2 is a threshold with a specific number attached,
so it is being amended rather than edited in place. G-1, G-3, the arms, the
seed, the step count, and the decision rule are all unchanged. Nothing in this
amendment touches the prediction that decides the experiment.
