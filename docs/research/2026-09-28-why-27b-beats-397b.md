# Why 27B Beats 397B — and What Transfers to 58M

Sources checked 2026-09-28. Purpose: the user asked why a 27B dense model
outscores a 397B MoE, and whether architecture is the cause. Answer: mostly no.
This document records what the frontier actually credits for these gains, and
which parts survive translation to a 58M model on 2× T4.

## The question

Qwen3.6-27B (dense, 27B) reports beating Qwen3.5-397B-A17B (397B total, 17B
active) on every major agentic coding benchmark: SWE-bench Verified 77.2 vs
76.2, SWE-bench Pro 53.5 vs 50.9, Terminal-Bench 2.0 59.3 vs 52.5, SkillsBench
48.2 vs 30.0. That is a ~15x parameter gap closed by a *dense* model against
an MoE. The obvious hypothesis is architecture. The evidence says otherwise.

## Decomposition: what actually produced the gain

| Source | Evidence | Weight in the result |
|---|---|---|
| **Data scale + curation** | Qwen3 pretraining went 18T → ~36T tokens, 119 languages; PDF text extracted with Qwen2.5-VL and rewritten with Qwen2.5; synthetic math/code from Qwen2.5-Math / -Coder | **Largest single contributor** |
| **Instance-level data mixture** | Qwen3 does not mix at the domain level: it annotates 30T+ tokens on educational value / field / domain / safety, then optimises the mixture **per instance** via ablations on small proxy models | **Largest methodological lever** |
| **Multi-stage pretraining** | Three stages, not one mixture: S1 general (~30T, 4K ctx) → S2 knowledge/reasoning-dense (~5T, STEM + code + reasoning, with *accelerated LR decay*) → S3 long-context (32K) | **Large, and directly copyable** |
| **Post-training (SFT + RL)** | All the "beats 397B" coding numbers are post-trained models. Four-stage pipeline: long-CoT cold start → reasoning RL (GRPO, 3,995 query-verifier pairs) → thinking/non-thinking fusion → general RL | **Large — and unavailable to us** |
| **Architecture** | Gated DeltaNet (GDN) hybrid with full attention every 4th layer; 4-branch Gated Residual; n-gram embedding tables (20V → 200V) held off-accelerator | **Marginal at our scale** |
| **Test-time compute** | Independent of model size; a 1B model with inference-time scaling beats a 405B model without it | **Large, and free to us** |

Corroborating evidence that architecture is downstream of data: Qwen3's own MoE
ablation finds MoE base models match dense base models at **1/5 activated
parameters — given the same pretraining data**. The sparsity is not what closed
the gap; the corpus and the schedule were.

Cross-family control (ThinkLM, 72 SLMs): Qwen2.5-7B beats Mistral-7B by >30% on
GSM8K. Same size, same architecture family, different data and post-training.
Conversely Hymba-1.5B, with a *more novel* architecture (hybrid attention+SSM),
scores 53.75% on GSM8K against Qwen2.5-1.5B's 70%. **Novel architecture without
strong training data underperforms a plain transformer with it.**

## The finding that should change how we optimise

Qwen3.8-Flash-Next tech report, verbatim: *"Loss and downstream accuracy do not
always move together."* Their documented cases:

- Widening the residual stream: **+1.58 points average accuracy**, loss gap only **0.002**.
- Making the residual read/write data-dependent: a further **+1.98 points**.
- Enlarging the n-gram vocabulary: loss falls **monotonically** while downstream
  accuracy **saturates or fluctuates**.
- Predicting residual read/write weights from the residual state: marginal loss
  reduction, clear benchmark gain.

Under a fixed parameter budget, their **loss optimum diverges from their accuracy
optimum.** A project that selects changes by val loss alone — which is exactly
what KALIA's protocol has done at every step so far — would discard several of
these. This is the strongest argument in the whole search for changing our
screening metric, and it is independent of model scale.

## The 1000x lever that actually exists: test-time compute

Not parameters. Compute *per question*.

- Snell et al. 2024: with compute-optimal test-time scaling, a small model
  **outperforms a 14x larger model** in a FLOPs-matched comparison; the method
  improves test-time efficiency **4x over best-of-N**. Caveat stated by the
  authors: on the *hardest* questions, pretraining compute wins; test-time wins
  on easy/intermediate prompts and at low inference-to-pretraining ratios.
- Snell et al. follow-up: a **1B model with inference-time scaling beats a 405B
  Llama 3** that lacks it.
- Hassid et al. 2024: 13B sampling 5x with unit-test selection beats one 70B
  sample by up to **15%** at equal FLOPs. Where no verifier exists, ranking-based
  selection **falls short** — the verifier is the load-bearing part.
- Snell et al. also: 4x improvements from simply not double-counting the
  reasoning already in the model.

**Why this is available to KALIA and distillation is not:** best-of-N with
self-consistency needs a *verifier*, not a teacher. KALIA's own log-probability
is a verifier it already owns. No foreign weights enter the model; D2
(from-scratch-only) is intact. Scaling in N is close to linear in useful work
extracted, and our 2× T4 generates N samples as cheaply as one.

Honest limits to record now: StyleBench finds search-based reasoning styles
(ToT, AoT) **fail on smaller models** and only help above a capacity threshold;
at 58M, the simple sampler-plus-scorer is the right shape and elaborate search
is not. And the same paper finds smaller models fail reasoning by breaking down
early, not by exhausting their token budget.

## What we are NOT taking, and why

- **MoE upcycling.** The `moe-upcycle` repo's own measured conclusion: "at this
  scale the upcycled model is bigger, slower, and slightly *worse* than the base
  it came from" (base 9.27 ppl vs upcycled 13.46 on Qwen2.5-0.5B). Liew et
  al.'s upcycling scaling law: "if no pretrained model exists, direct training is
  preferable to upcycling for optimal performance," and upcycling only wins while
  the extra token budget stays under a threshold D\* (4B tokens for 8×1B) —
  our *entire* remaining budget is 2.5B. Not a fit.
- **Distillation / post-training from a teacher.** Excluded by the user and by D2.
  Recorded here so the exclusion is visible rather than forgotten.
- **Long CoT + GRPO.** Qwen's reasoning RL used 3,995 query-verifier pairs and
  produced AIME 70.1 → 85.1 in 170 RL steps. At 58M with no verifier and no
  teacher, this is not reproducible; and the controlled reasoning study finds
  reasoning-format training needs ≥1.5B before it beats plain IFT, and *hurts*
  below that (0.5B students show catastrophic forgetting).
- **n-gram embedding tables.** Clever and nearly free in FLOPs, but it changes
  the output distribution and would break the canonical held-out set. Parked.

## The transfer plan (three compounding bets, not one 1000x bet)

| Version | Change | Source of expected gain | Cost | Yardstick |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **v0.2.1** | Three-stage corpus: 60% general → 30% reasoning-dense (math/code/logic) → 10% long-form fiction | Qwen S2 stage; long-range literature says continuous fiction is what buys LAMBADA | Same wall clock | **Byte-identical val.bin** |
| **v0.2.2** | Complete the schedule: `min_lr_ratio` 0.1 → ~0.01, steps set to affordable max | Qwen "accelerated LR decay"; our own D41 plateau was measured inside an *unfinished* cosine, so it is weak evidence | Same wall clock | Byte-identical |
| **v0.3.0** | Test-time compute: best-of-N with self-consistency (own log-prob as verifier), plus benchmark-not-just-loss screening | Test-time scaling literature; Qwen3.8's loss/accuracy divergence | Inference-time only | New eval, registered in advance |

## The honest-new-algorithm target

The Abhimanyu gap is **architectural**: 5.1 nats at 30M params, 6.06 at 58M.
X16 attacked it with a *data* transform and failed (D42) — which makes the
failure informative rather than embarrassing: the deficit is in the model's
ability to process a sequence in reverse, not in its exposure to reversed text.
Therefore the next hypothesis should target the **attention mechanism**, not the
data: a reverse-pass-aware or bidirectionally-gated attention bias, so that
reverse-order information is architecturally reachable rather than merely
predicted. A Sanskrit-motivated framing is legitimate here, but the discipline is
what matters: pre-register the prediction and threshold, run it at micro scale,
and publish it whether it passes or fails. Three honest failures are worth more
than one success we cannot explain.
